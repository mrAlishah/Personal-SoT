# research

## purpose

Provide reusable evidence-acquisition and verification discipline for tasks that depend on external, current, or source-backed information.

## rules

1. Define what claims require verification and whether freshness materially affects correctness.
2. Prefer the strongest available evidence source for the claim:

```text
primary_or_official_source
>
high_quality_secondary_source
>
community_or_experiential_source
```

Use community evidence when experience, sentiment, or real-world usage is itself relevant.
3. For time-sensitive claims, verify against current dated evidence rather than relying on stale background knowledge.
4. Open and inspect the underlying source when practical; do not treat a search-result snippet as equivalent to source evidence.
5. Cross-check material claims when stakes, ambiguity, disagreement, or source limitations justify it.
6. Separate source-supported findings from inference, synthesis, and recommendation.
7. Preserve material dates, versions, jurisdictions, locations, and other qualifiers that affect applicability.
8. State uncertainty when evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or indirect.
9. Preserve traceability/citations for material externally derived claims when the client supports it.
10. Do not manufacture sources, quotes, dates, statistics, or consensus.
11. Prefer focused retrieval over broad collection; gather the minimum evidence needed to answer defensibly.
12. Stop searching when additional sources are unlikely to change the material conclusion or reduce important uncertainty.

## source_conflicts

When trustworthy sources conflict:

```text
identify the disagreement
compare authority, date, scope, and applicability
preserve unresolved conflict when it cannot be resolved safely
```

Do not silently average incompatible claims.

## boundaries
